Founders launching their first paid acquisition channel almost always ask the same question: Google Ads or Meta Ads first? The honest answer is that it depends on what kind of demand you're trying to reach — because the two platforms don't do the same job.

## The core difference: existing demand vs. created demand

**Google Ads** (Search, Shopping, Performance Max) captures demand that already exists. Someone searches "best project management software for agencies," and your ad shows up because you've bid on that intent. The customer already knows they have a problem and is actively looking for a solution.

**Meta Ads** (Facebook and Instagram) creates demand. You're not waiting for someone to search — you're interrupting their scroll with an offer they weren't necessarily looking for, based on interest and behavioral targeting. That's a fundamentally different psychological moment: convincing someone they have a problem worth solving right now, versus meeting someone who already knows it.

## When to start with Google Ads

- Your product or service has clear, searchable intent (people already type queries related to what you sell).
- You're B2B or high-consideration B2C, where buyers research before purchasing.
- You have a decent volume of relevant search terms with reasonable competition — check this before committing budget, since some categories have prohibitively expensive CPCs.

## When to start with Meta Ads

- You're D2C or ecommerce with a visually compelling product — Meta's ad formats reward strong creative.
- Your category doesn't have obvious high-intent search terms yet (a genuinely new product category, for instance).
- You need to build awareness and retargeting pools before search demand for your brand even exists.

## The honest answer for most brands

Most growing brands end up needing both, just not on day one. A common, low-risk sequencing:

1. Start with whichever platform matches your buyer's actual behavior (see above).
2. Get tracking right first — GA4, Meta Pixel, and Conversions API — before spending meaningfully on either. Bad tracking makes both platforms look worse than they are and leads to premature budget-cutting decisions based on incomplete data.
3. Layer in the second platform once the first is generating stable, measurable results — Meta's retargeting audiences get stronger once Google Ads has been sending qualified traffic to your site for a few weeks, and vice versa.

The mistake to avoid is running both from week one, splitting a small budget two ways, and drawing conclusions from underpowered data on either platform. Pick one, get conclusive signal, then expand.
